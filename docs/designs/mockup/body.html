Why it can zoom at all

The render is an orthographic front elevation, so every door is the same size and shape wherever it sits — doors 01 and 04 measure identically. Pushing in is then a scale and a slide, and the door stays square with its number readable.

In perspective the same push would swing the door and skew the number. That is why the three-quarter view was dropped.

The number is the real one

The amber used to be painted over the door and the number redrawn on top — bigger, flatter, in a different face, and dead-centre where the real stencil is not. It read as a sticker.

The tint is mix-blend-mode: color now: hue and saturation from the amber, luminance from the render. Nothing is covered, so the door's own number, its shadow and the specular on its top edge all survive being lit. The bloom is masked out of the door for the same reason — a blurred blob underneath is part of what the blend reads.

More than one box can be yours

Two parcels at one cabinet is a Tuesday, not an edge case — and it is the moment the flow is quietest about, because "which box opens when I scan?" has no answer in it.

Every door that is yours lights. The switch under the render carries the numbers and a dot for the urgent one; the ticket below it carries when that box was dropped and how long is left. Choosing moves the frame, the ticket and the button together, and all of it is built from one array, so no two of them can disagree.

Full has to be said out loud

The screen used to answer is there room here? by lighting nothing at all — and an unlit cabinet is also what this screen looks like while it is loading, while a request is in flight, and when something has gone wrong. Absence is not a message.

So the render is drained and dimmed, a badge sits on it in words, and the panel states the count. All three, because the dim alone could be a rendering fault and the badge alone leaves twenty doors looking perfectly available underneath it. The button becomes the way out — a disabled primary states the problem and offers nothing.

What changed, and why

Sign in was a form, not a screen

One phone field and a button. It said nothing about what the product is, and it made SMS the only way in. Rebuilt on your Stitch layout: the mark, three lines of large tight type with the last in the accent, the form on its own panel under the thumb.

Google, and the VGU account, are not decoration. Every SMS code costs money per message — a real line item for a student project, paid on every sign-in and every resend. Google is free, and VGU students already have an account. Phone stays as the third way in for anyone who wants it.

Apple sign-in is dropped even though Stitch has it. We are Android-first by ADR 0007, so it would be a button nobody on the target device can press.

Social sign-in needs one more screen

The shipper finds a receiver by typing a phone number on the cabinet. Sign in with Google and we have an email, not a number — so there is an "one more thing" screen that asks for it, and says why in one line.

This is a contract change: endpoints 1 and 2 assume phone-then-code. Social sign-in needs a token exchange, and a way to attach a phone number to an existing account. Free today because the contract has not gone to the Server team; expensive once it has.

Words cut

  • Scan13 words → 3. The aperture already says what to do
  • Opened7 → 5
  • Refusal13 → 4

Refusal wording is shared with the cabinet and fixed by api-contract.md, so shortening it is a contract change rather than a screen edit. Already done in the file.

The map shows a walk, not a place

Two hand-drawn versions of this were wrong — one invented outright, one read off satellite imagery by eye. It is not drawn now: the ways come from the Overpass API and are projected straight into the card. But the third version was wrong too, in a subtler way — it was framed round the whole campus, so the walk you would actually take occupied about a fifth of it. A map of a place is not a map of a journey.

The window is sized from the route now. The line is a real pedestrian route over real ways — valhalla1.openstreetmap.de, costing=pedestrian — from a building on campus to the gate node: 543 m, 6 min 32 s, which is where 7 min walk · 540 m comes from. It is computed once, at build time, and baked into the SVG, because a published page has no network at runtime.

A dot and a pin, not two dots. Two coloured markers at the ends of a line say these are both places; a dot and a pin say you are here, that is where you are going with no legend and no copy.

Tapping it hands off to Google Maps in walking mode, with the gate's coordinates as the destination. Nothing this app can draw beats the map application already on the phone for the last hundred metres — it has the live position, it re-routes when you turn the wrong way, and it talks. So the card does not try: it answers which gate, which side, how far at a glance and gets out of the way. No key, no SDK, no request from the app at all.

The pin is an OpenStreetMap barrier=gate node at 11.107471, 106.611139, tagged opening_hours: Mo-Su 06:00-23:00 — seven metres from the place Google labels Cổng phụ Trường Đại học Việt Đức. The other three gates inside the box are access=no or locked=yes, so this is the one a receiver can walk through.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL — geometry and routing both. Chosen over Google's tiles for exactly that reason: the tiles are artwork and cannot be redrawn from, the data is open and can. This credit belongs in the app's about screen once it has one.

Still open

Amber or blue for your door on the cabinet tab. Amber reads instantly on steel but it is the door-open colour, and using it here spends it early.

Vietnamese or English leads. The sign-in headline is Vietnamese with English support underneath, which is a bet, not a decision. PRODUCT.md still has this open.

Which way in is the default. Right now the phone field has focus and Google sits under a divider. If SMS cost is the deciding factor it should be the other way round.
